# v1: the site goes public

Scoped 2026-09-15. Target: **Friday 2026-09-18**, explicitly half baked.

`DECISIONS.md` is authoritative on conflict. This file is a plan and goes stale;
when it disagrees with a decision entry, the entry wins.

## Premise, restated because it gets violated section by section

The registry never designates; consumers pin, visibly. Nothing in this plan may
make one submission the answer for a label. Everything below is about making the
existing corpus legible to a stranger, not about ranking it, gating it, or
telling anyone which artifact is good.

## What v1 is, in one sentence

The site becomes public at controlbun.com, honest about being eight submissions
and a design, with a stranger able to tell within ten seconds what it is, whose
it is, and what happens next.

**v1 is not the indexed corpus, and indexing is no longer next either.** The
2026-09-14 decision that the corpus gets seeded by indexing the literature stands
on the books and is deferred, not reversed. The lane taken on 2026-09-15 is the one
without it. Friday ships the vessel, and the corpus stays at eight submissions
until either the author adds more of his own or somebody submits.

## A. Publication mechanics

These exist because the repo is going public. None is optional and all three
obligations were written down in advance, in `DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-13.

**A1. History audit at the tip of the day.** Publishing exposes every commit, not
the tip. The last audit was dated `07489dd` and the tip is now `77a155c`. Confirm
again that `_local/`, `.env`, `.env.*` and `NOTES-private.md` have never been
committed on any branch at any point, and that no tracked file or commit message
carries anything credential-shaped, personal, financial or immigration-related.
The standing rule is that those stay out of the repo entirely, and a public repo
is where that gets tested rather than trusted.

**A2. Amend the repo section of `CLAUDE.md`.** Done 2026-09-16. The no-public-push
prohibition is gone; the no-hosted-build one is not, and the reason it existed is
unchanged, so Pages deploys a locally built `astro/dist` from a branch. What is
served is then the artifact that passed `make verify`, rather than whatever a
runner produced from the same source, and an Actions build would break that
equivalence quietly.

**A3. GitHub Pages, deploying from a branch.** `astro.config.mjs` needs `site`
set to the final URL. A `CNAME` file for controlbun.com in the published output.
DNS at the registrar: four A records to GitHub's apex addresses, or a CNAME on
`www`. Confirm HTTPS provisions before announcing anything.

**A4. Name registrations, same sitting.** The name going public is the trigger
already recorded: npm `controlbun`, PyPI `controlbun`, the GitHub org. The
trademark intent-to-use was deferred about a month and that deferral was measured
from the name going public, so Friday starts that clock rather than extending it.

## B. What a stranger needs, from the blind first-visit report

`_local/first-visit/REPORT.md`, ranked by what it cost the visitor rather than by
what is easy. The lede and the plurality paragraph both landed, so the home page
prose is not the problem and does not get rewritten.

**B1. The sidebar names three needs and turns all three off.** Getting started,
Documentation, Publishing a submission, all marked not in v0. In the visitor's
words: "the site listed my exact needs by name, and marked them unavailable. I
had nowhere to go but the sample data, and the sample data is mostly fake by the
site's own admission. This is the thing that made me stop." Highest cost in the
report and among the cheapest to fix.

**B2. Nothing says what the site is for or what it wants.** The visitor worked
out that nothing was possible here by accumulating five separate refusals across
five pages. One short, unapologetic statement of stage and intent, in one place,
replaces five scattered apologies. This is prose and it is the author's to write.

**B3. Nobody signs it.** `/about/` says "I" throughout and never says who. No
contact, no affiliation, no outbound link anywhere on the site. "Whose is it?"
was one of the questions the visitor left with, and on a public site under a
claimed name it is the one that matters most.

**B4. The 404 throws away the site.** Astro's unstyled default: dark monospace,
no header, no navigation, no way home, against a light serif site. The visitor
hit it twice, once on nonsense and once by trimming a URL the way a
GitHub-shaped URL trains you to. Being lost is exactly when the header matters.

**B5. The ordering paragraph repeats above the content on every list page.** By
the third page the visitor was skipping the top of pages on reflex, which is a
habit that then costs every other thing at the top of a page. The 100 threshold
is also stated everywhere and explained nowhere.

Not in scope, and deliberately: the jargon (B6 in the report) and the four
uninterpretable numbers. Both are real, both need a glossary or a change in what
the artifact page assumes, and neither is a Friday job. The refusal to grade a
score is working as designed and the visitor understood it by the end.

## C. Carried over, not blocking

From the code review, `_local/code-review/REPORT.md`. Nothing here blocks a
read-only public site, which is why none of it is in section A or B.

- **S1**, no content hash on fetched bytes. Needs migration 006 and verification
  in `client.vector()`. The largest open item and the first thing after v1.
- **S2**, `hub_url` validates only the commit, so a repo string containing `?`
  silently drops the pin. Latent until there is an upload path.
- **C4**, a zero-norm vector writes `NaN` into the export and breaks the build.
  Low probability, cheap to guard, would be an embarrassing way to fail.
- **T2, T4, T5, T6**, four tests with real holes, including one that degrades to
  a silent skip if a label or version is renamed.
- **C6**, the falsifier still cannot see the similarity numbers a reader actually
  reads, because they are injected client-side.

## Risks, named rather than managed

**The dual-use override is the real one.** `DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-13 records the
policy as the launch blocker, in the words "the site going up is the launch", and
v1 overrides it knowingly. Going public compounds it: `BRIEF.md` and
`VALIDATION.md` become public, and both carry the project's own extended analysis
of misuse surface, refusal-removal artifacts and the tension between open
contribution and misuse gating. A reader finds the analysis and finds no policy
answering it, which reads worse than a quiet site with no policy at all. The
mitigation, if one is wanted later, is a single page: what the registry will and
will not hold, that it serves no weights today, and how to report something.

**Pagefind is a discovery surface.** `astro/dist/pagefind/` indexes every page's
body text, which includes definitions, labels and attack methods. Over eight
entries that is nothing. It is worth knowing before the corpus is a few hundred.

**Seven of eight submissions are fabricated.** The banner says so, and the
visitor called that banner the reason they trusted everything else. But a public
site whose corpus is seven-eighths synthetic invites "is this real", which was on
the visitor's list of things they left not knowing. Not a defect to hide; a fact
to state plainly in B2.

**Every real submission is by one author, on one label, on one model.** The
plurality thesis is currently demonstrated only by fixtures, and the lane without
indexing keeps it that way until contribution works or the author publishes more
of his own arena directions. That was the strongest argument for indexing next, and
deferring indexing does not answer it, it postpones it. Worth re-reading before
anyone shows this to a skeptical reader as evidence of the thesis.

## After v1, in the order I would take them

The lane without indexing, decided 2026-09-15. Indexing sits behind all of this
rather than in front of it.

1. **The five-person question**, because it is a week of email and it is the only
   work that moves the one number nobody has: whether anyone wants this. Without
   indexing, the corpus only grows if people submit, so the answer determines
   whether the contribution path is worth building at all.
2. **More of the author's own arena directions.** Not indexing, not waiting: the
   arena holds directions at other layers and from other seasons. Adding them is
   the only lever in this lane that makes the site less of a demonstration, and it
   fixes one model and one label even though it cannot fix one author. Season 1 is
   Llama and its license is not Apache-2.0, so that is a separate check before
   rather than after.
3. **S1**, the content hash, which also has to land before any write path.
4. **The package.** `pip install controlbun` and a resolve call is how anyone
   actually uses this, and it is the only thing that produces support cards. In
   this lane it matters more, not less: with no indexed corpus, a person using
   `load()` against the three real directions is the thinnest real path to a first
   user that exists.
5. **The dual-use policy**, which gates the v2 review step and, whenever it
   returns, the indexer.
6. **v2 contribution.** `V2.md`.
